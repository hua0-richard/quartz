Suppose we have $P(X = x, Y = y)$. 

Denote as $f_{X,Y}​(x,y)$.

Then we must have
$$
\int \int f_{X,Y}​(x,y)dxdy=1
$$
> If the two random variables $X$ and $Y$ are **not independent**, we *cannot* write them as the product of $X$ and $Y$ distributions. We *must* be given the joint *pdf* $f(x, y)$.

In the case of $X$ and $Y$ being **independent**, then we can write the joint distribution as a product of their *pdfs*. 

$$
\int \int f_X(x)f_Y(y) dx dy
$$
